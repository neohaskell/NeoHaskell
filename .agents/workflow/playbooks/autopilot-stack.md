### Autopilot-stack

**You own the stack, never the landing. Hand the operator one linear stack of base-chained PRs to review and land.** **Autopilot-full** holds except for how verified work leaves. Each tick re-reads both playbooks from trunk, and this one wins where they differ.

1. **Run Autopilot-full's owners, minus the landing.** Owners report STACK-READY in place of merge-ready. They never rebase onto another branch, trunk included, and never retarget, merge, close a PR, or arm auto-merge, because the root owns the chain's topology and the operator owns the landing. When an item depends on another, its owner branches from that item's branch, not from trunk.
2. **Give the topology one writer.** Owners push only their own branches and report each tip and its intended parent. Only you rebase or retarget a link, because two writers on one chain undo each other. Append each clean PR in dependency order, with its base set to its parent's branch (`gh pr edit <pr> --base <parent-branch>`, or the supported integrated PR interface per `AGENTS.md` and Opening a PR). Before a topology rewrite or push, stop the owner's writes and record the handoff to the root. On a conflict, hand the slice back to its owner to resolve, then regain exclusive write ownership before pushing. Report the resulting head, parent/base and expected-head lease before the owner resumes. This announced handoff authorizes the root's recorded push; an unexpected push still stops the owner under Autopilot-full.
3. **Absorb drift at the root.** Until landing starts, apply Autopilot-full step 5's test to each link and rebase the chain bottom-up where it calls for one. Then apply `.agents/workflow/playbooks/shipping.md` step 3 link by link, since a link's base is its parent. Record the tested parent/head and relevant compiler, dependency, build-flag, generator, fixture and runtime inputs. After a rewrite, rerun affected lanes and carry forward unaffected evidence only with an independent explanation of unchanged inputs and coverage; a matching patch ID alone is not proof.
4. **Deliver the chain, then keep your hands off it.** Each link carries its verdict in its PR. Once landing starts, nobody writes to the chain except the operator-authorized post-merge child transition in `.agents/workflow/playbooks/shipping.md` step 4, including its recorded old-parent boundary, child-only replay onto actual merged trunk, retarget, expected-head lease and revalidation, because a second writer breaks a landing in flight. A mid-landing problem goes through you to the operator, and any fix waits for the operator's go.

**Choosing the execution workflow.** Use the [task completion policy](../../skills/orchestrate/references/task-completion.md) to establish the delivery boundary. Autopilot-full handles independent PRs when landing is in scope. Autopilot-stack handles an explicit operator-lands or no-merge request. Coupled work alone does not imply operator review or withheld landing. With landing in scope, run one dependent task through Autonomous run, or a standing program through Orchestrate, preserving its real PR stack and Shipping checks.

**Reply:** catch the operator up per the **ketchup** skill, with links to the stack root and tip, a one-line verdict per link, and anything parked or excluded with the reason.
