Your shop starts with a simple promise: a customer asks for a mug, and you deliver
what you agreed. Software makes that promise harder to follow when payments,
stock, emails, and people all act at different times.

NeoHaskell gives those actions and their consequences a shared structure. You can
model what should happen, work with a coding agent to implement it, and check the
result against the business you understand.

Each page begins with the situation and becomes more detailed as you read. You can
stop after understanding the business decision, continue into implementation, or
return later for a specific answer. [How to use these docs](/start/) explains the
paths and the difference between a working example and an exercise.

The examples grow around a fictional mug shop. The executable foundations are the
public NeoHaskell starter and reference application. Payment, shipping, and other
shop-specific policies are identified as designs you implement; they are not a
prebuilt commerce product hidden behind the tutorial.
